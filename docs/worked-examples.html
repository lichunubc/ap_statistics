Topic 1.6 Descriptions for One Quantitative Variable Distributions

Describe the whole distribution, not just its average. Connect shape, center, spread and unusual features to the variable and its context.

Describe a distribution · Worked examples and evidence

Describe a distribution

The four-part description

Part Ask yourself Include in the response
Shape Symmetry or skew? How many main peaks? Overall balance and prominent peaks
Center Where is a central value? A numerical location, with units
Spread How widely do values vary? An interval or overall span, with units
Unusual features Gaps, clusters, or potential outliers? Approximate locations and evidence

Always name the variable, group, and units. Write “commute times among these 24 students,” not just “the graph.” A description is more than a list of vocabulary words.

Skew is named for the longer tail

Right-skewed distributions have a longer tail toward larger values. Left-skewed distributions have a longer tail toward smaller values. Approximately symmetric means the sides roughly mirror each other. The tallest bars do not identify the direction of skew.

Right-skewed illustration: many smaller values, with a tail toward larger values.

Left-skewed illustration: many larger values, with a tail toward smaller values.

Peaks answer a different question

Unimodal means one main peak; bimodal means two prominent peaks. Approximately uniform means similar frequencies across equal-width intervals, without prominent peaks. Two adjacent tall bars may form one broad peak; not every small bump is a separate mode.

Compare these shapes: symmetric with one broad peak; symmetric and bimodal; approximately uniform.

A distribution may be symmetric and bimodal. Symmetric does not necessarily mean bell-shaped.

Shape detective: published data

Inspect each histogram before choosing A–E. These are the published datasets from the revised lecture, not repeated versions of the schematic examples. Approximate descriptions allow ordinary irregularities in real data.

Cases: major North American river lengths, Old Faithful waiting times, and Iris setosa sepal lengths. Source links appear with each case.

Center and spread are different

The next group has 30 practice-task times. Its balance is easy to see near 20 minutes. Observations extend from 12 to 28 minutes, giving an overall span of 28−12=1628-12=16 minutes.

Symmetric task-time data centered at 20 minutes; minimum 12, maximum 28.

“About 20 minutes is central” answers a different question from “values span 16 minutes.” Do not use the height of the tallest bar as the center: that height is a count, not a value of the variable.

Clusters, gaps, and potential outliers

Clusters are concentrations of observations. A gap is an interval between observed values containing no observations. An outlier is unusually small or large relative to the rest; a graph may identify a potential outlier for investigation.

Clinic times cluster at 5–12 and 24–30 minutes, with an isolated value at 55 minutes.

Here, 55 minutes is unusually separated from the main groups. It is not automatically a recording error and should not automatically be deleted. A single high-end observation does not, by itself, require the outlier label.

TipA useful sentence frame

“Among [group], the distribution of [variable and units] is [shape], centered around [value or interval], and extends from about [lower end] to [upper end]. [Describe any notable gap, cluster, or unusual value.]”

Use approximate language for graphical estimates. With two separated concentrations, explain both instead of treating a central value in the gap as a typical observation.

Worked examples and evidence

Describe the commute histogram

Use only this grouped display. Remembering the raw list is different from reading evidence from a histogram.

Six 10-minute bins starting at zero have frequencies 2, 8, 7, 4, 2, 1.

The shape is roughly unimodal and right-skewed. The center is approximately in the 20–30 minute interval. Occupied bins extend from 0–10 through 50–60 minutes. There is no empty 10-minute bin and just one observation in the last bin.

Among these 24 students, recorded commute times are roughly unimodal and right-skewed, centered in the 20–30 minute interval. Values occupy bins from 0 to under 10 through 50 to under 60 minutes. No displayed bin is empty, although smaller gaps may be hidden inside bins.

Do not report an exact minimum of 5 or maximum of 58 from this graph alone. Those facts require the individual observations.

Support a claim numerically

Claim: Most of these students recorded commute times under 30 minutes.

2+8+7=17 students,1724×100%≈70.8%.2+8+7=17\text{ students},\qquad \frac{17}{24}\times100\%\approx70.8\%.

Seventeen is more than half of 24, so the claim is supported. Write: “About 71% of these sampled students recorded a commute time under 30 minutes.” Do not silently extend the conclusion to every student at the school.

Evidence check

Decide what the histogram alone establishes. Explain which part of the graph supports your answer.

Four statements test bin counts, exact values, majority, and the exact maximum.

A threshold inside a bin

Exactly 2+1=32+1=3 times are at least 40 minutes. But at least 45 minutes splits the 40–50 bin. Its two observations could lie below or above 45; the one in 50–60 definitely qualifies. Therefore between 1 and 3 students could qualify from the histogram alone. Do not assume uniformity inside a bin.

Dissolved oxygen in cold streams

2023 AP Statistics, Question 1(a).

Worked solution

The variable is dissolved oxygen concentration, in mg/L, for sampled Alaskan streams colder than 8°C.

One-unit bins from 2 to 14 mg/L have frequencies 1, 0, 1, 1, 13, 12, 15, 40, 94, 117, 89, 46; total 429.

Feature Evidence
Shape One peak at 11–12 mg/L; tail toward smaller values
Center About 11–12 mg/L
Spread Occupied bins extend across roughly 2–14 mg/L
Unusual features Empty 3–4 bin; potentially unusual low values below 6 mg/L
Show the complete model response

For these sampled Alaskan streams colder than 8°C, dissolved oxygen concentrations are unimodal and left-skewed, centered around 11–12 mg/L. Occupied bins extend across roughly 2–14 mg/L, with a gap in the 3–4 mg/L bin and several potentially unusual low measurements below 6 mg/L. The boundaries do not reveal the exact minimum and maximum.

Follow-up calculation

The total frequency is 429. The four bins from 10 to under 14 mg/L contain

94+117+89+46=346,346429×100%≈80.7%.94+117+89+46=346,\qquad \frac{346}{429}\times100\%\approx80.7\%.

About 81% of these sampled cold streams occupy those bins. Height 117 means 117 observations in the whole 11–12 mg/L interval, not exactly 11. Use left-inclusive, right-exclusive intervals for this calculation.

Recover values from a dotplot

2022 AP Statistics: chocolate study.

Two dotplots show recorded reductions in blood pressure in mmHg.

Read one entry per dot, including stacked dots. What are the two sorted lists?

Show the recovered lists

Dark Chocolate (13 observations):

-3, -1, 0, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 10, 12, 16

White Chocolate (12 observations):

-12, -6, -3, -3, -2, -2, 2, 3, 4, 4, 5, 15

Units are mmHg of reduction. Negative reduction means blood pressure increased; zero means no change. The order is sorted, not the original observation order. Each dot contributes one observation, including repeated values.
