Sources and study resources

Basis of the pilot

This book adapts Chun Li’s revised 45-slide Seeing Quantitative Data presentation and 30-page Representing and Describing Quantitative Data study guide for Maple Hill School. It retains the revised sequence, classroom datasets, whole-bar construction, endpoint convention, and evidence-based descriptions.

Book section Revised lecture slides
Individual values 3–9
Histogram construction and laboratory 10–19
Hand construction and data recovery 20–23
Shape, center, spread, unusual features 24–32
Descriptions and supported claims 33–37
Oxygen and closing practice 38–45

The scope follows the instructor-supplied College Board essential-knowledge excerpts 1.5.A.1–4, 1.6.A.1–6, and 1.6.B.1. Detailed numerical-summary formulas and inference are left to later lessons.

Published datasets

The shape challenge uses the values packaged with the revised lecture. Their documented sources are:

  • R datasets: rivers: 141 major North American river lengths in miles, a historical compilation identified as the 1975 World Almanac.
  • R datasets: faithful: 272 waiting times between Old Faithful eruptions, in minutes.
  • R datasets: iris: the 50 Iris setosa sepal lengths, in centimeters, from Anderson’s iris measurements.

These are published observations, not freshly simulated data. Other classroom datasets are explicitly hypothetical. Bin choices are teaching decisions, not properties of the observations.

Exam connections

2023 AP Statistics, Question 1(a): the official question, PDF page 4 supplies the oxygen histogram. This book redraws its numerical frequencies and provides classroom explanations and follow-ups, not an official scoring key.

2022 AP Statistics, Question 5: chocolate values are the revised lecture’s numerical transcription of the instructor-supplied dotplots. The public site does not upload the original screenshot or exam text. Consult College Board’s exam archive for currently available materials; older exam availability may differ.

Printing and downloads

Your browser’s Print / Save as PDF command saves a chapter. Printing expands solutions and displays completed construction examples. The screen version keeps solutions hidden until you open them.

Download the commute values as CSV. Editable .qmd source, a portable preview builder, Quarto configuration, tests, and publishing instructions are included in the project.

Privacy and limitations

There are no accounts, analytics scripts, advertisements, grades database, or student-submission service. Activity choices remain in the current page and disappear on refresh; they are not sent to a teacher. Normal hosting logs are separate: GitHub documents IP logging for Pages security.

JavaScript enables the activities. Core prose, tables, and solution text remain available without it. Charts use bundled data and do not request a remote statistical service. Search operates on the hosted site or a locally served copy; browser restrictions may affect search when opening a downloaded file directly.

Technical format

The publishing source is a Quarto HTML book with chapter navigation, search, mathematical typesetting, and custom JavaScript/SVG activities. It needs no running notebook kernel or paid backend. The downloadable portable preview uses Pandoc with the same chapter text and activities; the automated Quarto build produces the hosted book.
