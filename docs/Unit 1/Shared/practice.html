Heights are 3, 7, 10, 6, 3, 1, totaling 30. Every bar is 5 minutes wide. The tallest bar means ten waits from 10 to under 15 minutes—not ten waits exactly equal to 10.

2 · Stem, leaf, and key

Construct a stem-and-leaf plot for 1.2, 1.5, 1.5, 2.0, 2.3 seconds. Explain the repeated leaf.

Show solution 
1 | 2 5 5
2 | 0 3
Key: 1 | 2 = 1.2 seconds

Five leaves represent five observations. The repeated 1.5 needs two leaves of 5. A zero leaf preserves the 2.0-second observation.

3 · Bin boundaries

Which 10-minute commute bin contains 20? Does the first occupied bin prove the recorded minimum is 0?

Show solution

20 enters 20≤x<3020\leq x<30, not the preceding bin. The first occupied bin establishes a value in 0≤x<100\leq x<10, not necessarily 0. The original list—not the histogram—identifies the minimum as 5.

4 · Repair turnaround times

Describe the histogram of 40 bicycle repairs. Find the percentage taking at least 4 hours. Can you recover the most common exact time?

One-hour bins from 0 to 8: frequencies 12, 10, 7, 5, 3, 2, 1, 0.

Show solution

Repair times are unimodal and right-skewed, centered around 1–3 hours. Values occupy bins from 0 to under 7 hours. No displayed internal bin is empty. The one observation in 6–7 hours is not automatically an outlier; an empty final 7–8 bin is not an internal gap.

At least 4 hours: (3+2+1)/40=15%(3+2+1)/40=15\%. The most frequent interval is 0–1 hour. The most common exact value is not recoverable.

5 · Two concentrations

Describe shape, center, spread, and the empty internal interval for these 40 library study sessions. What percentage lasted from 30 to under 90 minutes?

Fifteen-minute bins from 0 to 120: frequencies 2, 10, 8, 0, 0, 8, 10, 2.

Show solution

The displayed distribution is approximately symmetric and bimodal, with main concentrations in 15–45 and 75–105 minutes. The visual center near 60 minutes lies in a gap, not at a typical observed session. Occupied bins span roughly 0–120 minutes. There are no observations from 45 to under 75 minutes.

The count from 30 to under 90 is 8+0+0+8=168+0+0+8=16, giving 16/40=40%16/40=40\%. A single center would hide the two concentrations. The exact median is not available from the histogram.

6 · What could be inside a bin?

Two commute times lie in 40–50 and one in 50–60 minutes. How many are at least 40? How many could be at least 45?

Show solution

Exactly 3 are at least 40. Between 1 and 3 could be at least 45: the last-bin observation definitely qualifies; either, neither, or both observations in the preceding bin might qualify. Do not add a uniformity assumption.

7 · Oxygen: count and context

Return to the oxygen histogram. How many sampled cold streams recorded below 6 mg/L? What percentage is this? What does the empty 3–4 bin establish?

Show solution

The first four counts total 1+0+1+1=31+0+1+1=3. The percentage is 3/429×100%≈0.7%3/429\times100\%\approx0.7\%. Under our convention, no observed concentration lies in 3≤x<43\leq x<4 mg/L. This does not show that such concentrations are impossible in all streams.

8 · Repair a weak description

A student writes, “The graph is high in the middle and goes up and down.” Replace this with all four parts of a contextual description.

Twenty review-session lengths span 4–30 minutes, with approximate symmetry and a broad central concentration.

Show a model response

Among these 20 students, review-session lengths are approximately symmetric with one broad central concentration, centered around 16–18 minutes. Recorded values extend from 4 to 30 minutes, with no clearly isolated observation or large internal gap.

The dotplot preserves individual values, so the observed endpoints are available. Wide histogram bins would warrant less exact wording.
