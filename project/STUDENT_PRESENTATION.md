# Student-facing presentation

Updated from Chun Li's explicit review of Topic 1.9 on 2026-10-15. Apply these rules to all existing and future chapters. They supersede earlier authoring instructions about displaying curriculum mappings, provenance commentary, question metadata, and interface design terminology to students. Preserve the underlying mathematics, objective coverage, source records, question IDs and tests.

1. Start a chapter with its official topic title and teaching content. Do not display a CED learning-targets/essential-knowledge/required-skills block at the top. Maintain complete curriculum mappings in `objectives.json` and editorial documentation, not in the student-facing introduction.
2. Keep dataset provenance in the data/source files. Remove repeated statements that data are original, hypothetical, simulated, not student records, or unrelated to earlier datasets from the main reading path. If a source note is useful, put a short note in a collapsed Sources section at the very bottom. Do not misrepresent fabricated observations as collected observations; simply introduce the scenario without making real-world provenance claims.
3. Write all mathematical expressions in valid Quarto LaTeX (prefer `$...$` for inline and `$$...$$` for display). Check the rendered MathML, including worked numeric substitutions, boxed results, and equations within derivation disclosures. Avoid raw `\\(...\\)` text and plain-text algebra where a typeset equation is intended. Dynamic practice math must render properly too.
4. Keep released exam questions and the walkthrough together on the lesson page. Chun Li has stated that he has College Board and Maple Hill School permission to reuse their assets for this educational website. Use the relevant original released-question page images where authorized; retain a compact source/year/question caption and accessible text equivalent. Record the instructor's permission statement as user-reported permission in source metadata, without claiming independent legal verification. This does not change the handling of secure AP Classroom material or publish the private homework bank. Do not upload font files or complete exam PDFs as part of this presentation revision.
5. Remove routine publishing/legal/authorship/old-versus-new-exam-format warnings from the student reading path, including commentary such as 'independently written', 'consult the official scoring guidelines for actual scoring', and notices that a cited question predates an exam-format change. Retain concise source attribution. Preserve substantive mathematical conditions and statistical reasoning (positive SD, appropriate denominators, limits of boxplots, association versus causation); these are lesson content, not administrative disclaimers.
6. Name the final practice section simply **Practice**. The one-question-at-a-time design is an implementation requirement, not part of its title or a paragraph describing the instructor's prompt. Useful controls remain Previous, Next question, Check answer, Hint, Solution, Reset and the question counter.
7. Hide difficulty labels, internal item IDs, skill tags, objective codes, and bank-storage explanations from student question cards, solutions and printed practice. Retain them in the structured problem-bank source for editing and reuse. Keep neutral question titles and all interaction/accessibility features.
8. In Topic 1.9 specifically, format Common errors and final checklist as readable bullets instead of a dense succession of 'do not' sentences. This is not a rule to turn every section in every chapter into a list.
9. Optional mathematical derivations remain expandable and labelled **Optional derivation** or **Optional enrichment** when outside the required course. Avoid repeated long disclaimers. Required conceptual explanations stay in the main lesson.
